# R19-hebrew-yiddish: two more languages of New York, in the Hebrew script

**Paul, 2026-10-12:** *"Can we add Yiddish and Hebrew."* The site speaks nineteen languages; these are the twentieth and twenty-first, `he` and `yi`, both right to left (`RTL` in `src/kit/core/i18n.ts` names them already), both in the Hebrew script. Everything in `directive-lang-translate.md` and `directive-lang2-translate.md` holds (read both in full first: the file's form, the voice, what stays untranslated, the length rule, the checks, and the New York rule), then `docs/translation.md`.

## The two voices

- **Hebrew (`he`).** The Hebrew of the city's Israeli and Hebrew-speaking communities, as consumer software in Israel addresses people today: direct and plain, the computing words people use (קובץ, קישור, הורדה, but also the English loanwords where Israeli software keeps them), and the forms that address nobody as a man or a woman where the English is an instruction or a button: the infinitive (להוריד, לשתף) or a noun, as Israeli interfaces do, never a masculine imperative to everyone. Say in the file's head what you chose. Numbers and dates as `Intl` gives `he`; the plural forms Hebrew needs (`npm run i18n:gaps -- he` prints each key with its forms).
- **Yiddish (`yi`).** The Yiddish that is a daily language in this city, which is the Hasidic communities' (Williamsburg, Borough Park, Crown Heights, and Kiryas Joel up the river): everyday words and the English loanwords they use for computing, in the spelling those communities write today where it differs from YIVO's standard (the Hebrew-origin words in their traditional spelling, the vowel letters as the community writes them, no YIVO diacritics except where a word is unreadable without one), the דו of software that speaks to one person as those communities' software and papers do, or איר where the community's register for a stranger asks it: decide, say why in the head, and keep it throughout. The report's `ambiguous` names every place where YIVO and the community differ and what you chose, so Paul can move it. Yiddish's plural forms by `npm run i18n:gaps -- yi`; `Intl` has thin data for `yi` in some browsers (check what `new Intl.DateTimeFormat('yi')` and `Intl.PluralRules('yi')` give in Chromium and WebKit here, and say in the report; where a browser falls back to the root locale the kit's own plural rules must still be right).
- Keep as they are, as every language does: "Tiny Little Office", "Aboard Foundry", "GitHub", app names where the earlier directives say so, the format's keywords, placeholders and plural syntax.

## The type

- **IBM Plex Sans Hebrew** (`@ibm/plex-sans-hebrew` 1.1.0 on npm, OFL-1.1): its Regular and SemiBold woff2, copied as released into `public/fonts/` (the package fetched with `npm pack @ibm/plex-sans-hebrew@1.1.0` into a scratch directory outside the repository and the two files taken from it; nothing added to package.json), listed in `scripts/dependencies.ts` (`FONTS`, with `docs/dependencies.md` regenerated by its script), declared in `src/styles/fonts.css` beside the Arabic faces with the Hebrew ranges (`U+0590-05FF, U+FB1D-FB4F`), and named in `public/_headers`'s font rules if the Arabic file is named there. The Yiddish diacritics (U+05B0-05C7 within the block) are in the same face. The Serif and Mono looks have no Hebrew face from IBM: say so in the report; the system's faces draw them, as they do for Arabic.
- The service worker's install list and the smoke run's request count are unchanged: the face waits behind its range like the others, and `scripts/fonts-box.sh` is run again so the box measures in it.
