# R19-agent-embeds: an agent learns that a document goes inside a document

**Paul, 2026-10-12**, from a claude.ai session he had make a deck about badgers: the model first did not believe the site existed and had to be told to look; then it wrote, verbatim, *"Tiny Little Office decks are text-only (icon, headline, bullets), so the visuals are companion links you can open alongside"* and *"Decks can't embed animations, and the Animator's text format isn't published."* It made four separate documents (a bar chart, a trip map, a diagram, a timeline) and linked them from bullets, and put a car icon where it wanted a moving go-kart.

Every one of those sentences is wrong about the product. A fence named by an app's id, ```` ```anim ```` to ```` ``` ````, puts that app's document on a slide, in a Word Processor page or under an outline item, drawn there by its own app and edited in place (`docs/tlo.md` "Fences the kit knows"; `src/apps/deck/view.ts` `subPage`; `src/apps/write/editor.ts` the figure). An animation moves on its slide (only the rail's thumbnails stand still, `src/apps/deck/editor.ts`). The Animator's format is at https://tinylittleoffice.com/llms/anim.txt, linked from llms.txt, which itself carries a complete Lift off link.

The guide is what undersold it, measured on main (3d63182):

- `public/llms.txt` (`scripts/make-skill.ts`, cut from `skill/tinyoffice/SKILL.tpl.md`): rule 2 says "a slide with a chart, a table or a diagram shows it alone" and nowhere says how a diagram gets onto a slide. The word "fence" appears once, about a sheet's chart.
- `public/llms/deck.txt`: one sentence, "A sheet's table with `chart: bar` in its head, in a ```` ```sheet ```` fence, is a chart on the slide." Nothing about any other app. `llms/write.txt` and `llms/outline.txt` say nothing about a fence at all.
- The shapes (`scripts/shapes.ts`): the deck's fourteen shapes have `chart` and no shape for a document on a slide; the Word Processor's have none for a figure; the Outliner's none for a document under an item. Since every example is cut from a template, the templates are the cause: of the Presentation Builder's twenty-six templates one holds a fence (a chart); the Word Processor's and the Outliner's hold none. A model that learns the format from the examples learns that nothing goes inside anything.
- The home page as a fetcher reads it (`scripts/seo.ts`, the words before any script): the apps' names, the tagline, then last of all "Agents: https://tinylittleoffice.com/llms.txt says how to make a document as a link." A fetcher that reads the first few hundred characters never reaches it.

## The design

1. **llms.txt says it, early.** A section "A document inside a document" after the one-example list and before the three rules: any app's document goes inside a Word Processor page, a slide or an outline item as a fence named by the app's id, drawn there by that app and edited in place; a slide with one shows it alone under the headline; an animation moves on its slide and plays when presented; a page's and a deck's own files (PDF, PowerPoint, Word) carry it drawn. One example link beside it: a two-slide deck whose second slide holds a short Animator document (the Lift off rocket, or smaller), under 600 characters. The whole file stays under 6 KB (`test/llms.test.ts` holds the size; cut elsewhere if it must, never the three rules).
2. **Each app's page says what it can hold.** `llms/deck.txt`, `llms/write.txt` and `llms/outline.txt` each say, in one sentence, that a fence named by any app's id (`sheet`, `draw`, `diagram`, `anim`, `trip`, `cal`, `calc`, the lot) puts that document there, where the deck page today names the sheet's chart alone. The source is `skill/tinyoffice/SKILL.tpl.md`; `npm run skill` regenerates llms.txt, llms-full.txt, the app pages and the skill; `npm run commands` whatever else is generated from it. The skill's own body gains the same section.
3. **Shapes, and the templates that show them.** `scripts/shapes.ts`: the deck gains `moves` ("a slide that moves: an Animator document in a ```anim fence") and `shows` ("a slide that shows a document: a diagram, a drawing, a map or a calendar in a fence named by its app"); the Word Processor gains `figure` ("a figure in the text: a drawing, a diagram, a chart or a map in a fence named by its app"); the Outliner gains `doc` ("a document under an item: a fence named by its app, {depth=n} for its place"). Each needs a template that shows it, since examples are cut from templates and nothing is hand-written: in the Presentation Builder, A lesson (the seventh-grade moon lesson) gains a slide where the model moves, an Animator document of the phases, and A proposal's "when" becomes a timeline diagram on its slide; in the Word Processor one template gains a figure where its words already describe one (Meeting notes' room, Memo's plan: the builder chooses and says which, with a diagram or a drawing, never a picture); in the Outliner one template gains a document under an item. Every added document is small (the Animator one under 400 characters), lints clean in its own app, and keeps the template's voice. `test/shapes.test.ts` (or where shapes are tested): every shape has an example, and `write(parse(x)) === x` for each.
4. **A lint for the mistake itself.** `src/lint.ts` (every app, since the kernel sees every block): a paragraph or a bullet holding a link to a Tiny Little Office document (`https://tinylittleoffice.com/<app>/#doc=` or `#/v1/<app>/`, or a short link of the site's own origin) gets the finding `doc-link`: "a link to a Tiny Little Office document in the words: to show the document here, put its text in a fence named by its app". Level: the same as the other advisory findings. The command line and the API say it the same way (they run the same lint). A test in `test/lint.test.ts` with a slide of companion links, and one showing the fence form is clean.
5. **The fetcher's first line.** In the page's pre-script words (`scripts/seo.ts`), the agents' sentence comes first, before the apps' names, and names both guides: "Agents: https://tinylittleoffice.com/llms.txt says how to make a document as a link; a document goes inside another as a fence named by its app." `test/seo.test.ts` holds the order.

## Proof

- `npm run skill` and `npm run commands` leave the generated files current (the tests that compare them); `test/llms.test.ts`: llms.txt holds the section, is under 6 KB, and its new example link opens in the deck app as a deck whose second slide's `subPage` is drawn by the Animator (a node test over `parse` and `subPage`, with frames above one); each of the three app pages names ```` ```anim ````.
- The four shapes with their examples in `packages/doc/examples/*.json`, cut from the templates named; `npx tinylittleoffice example deck moves` prints one.
- The lint test; `npx vitest run`; `npm run build`; the e2e smoke.
- The report says, with the before and after bytes of llms.txt and each app page, what an agent reading llms.txt alone now learns about embedding in one read.

## Not this

- No change to how a fence is drawn, edited or presented: the product does this today. No pictures, no fetching. No new app. No sentence in the guide that is not true of the product (the lint and the tests are the check, not a reading). The three rules stay the three rules.
